Phrasal Verbs (الأفعال المركبة)

Phrasal verbs are combinations of a verb + preposition/adverb that create a new meaning. They are very common in English and appear frequently in BAC exams.

Separable vs. Inseparable

Separable: The object can go between the verb and the particle.

I turned the offer down. / I turned down the offer.
She picked her son up. / She picked up her son.

With pronouns (it, him, her, them), the object MUST go between:

✓ I turned it down.
✗ I turned down it.

Inseparable: The object always comes after the particle.

✓ I look after my sister.
✗ I look my sister after.

BAC Exam Tips

  • Phrasal verbs are more common in informal English. Use them in dialogues and informal letters.
  • In formal essays, prefer one-word alternatives (postpone instead of put off, reject instead of turn down).
  • Learning phrasal verbs in context is easier than memorizing lists.
