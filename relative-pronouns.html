Relative Pronouns (الضمائر الموصولة)

Relative pronouns connect clauses and refer to nouns mentioned earlier. They are essential for complex sentences in BAC writing.

Complete Table

PronounUsed forExample
whoPeople (subject)The student who won the prize is my friend.
whomPeople (object, formal)The man whom I met was very kind.
whichThings, animals, ideasThe book which I bought is very interesting.
thatPeople or things (defining only)The painting that hangs on the wall is beautiful.
whosePossession (people & things)The student whose father is a doctor works hard.
wherePlacesThe city where I was born is Fes.
whenTimeThe day when we met was unforgettable.
whyReasonTell me the reason why you left.

Defining vs. Non-defining Relative Clauses

Defining (Restrictive) (الموصولة المحددة)

Gives essential information — without it, the sentence meaning changes. No commas.

The student who sits next to me is Ahmed.
(There are many students; I'm specifying which one.)

Non-defining (Non-restrictive) (الموصولة غير المحددة)

Gives extra information — the sentence works without it. Uses commas. CANNOT use "that".

My brother, who lives in Rabat, is a doctor.
(I have one brother; the information is extra.)

"Whose" for Possession

"Whose" shows ownership and can be used for both people and things.

The woman whose car was stolen called the police.
The company whose products are eco-friendly is successful.

Omitting the Relative Pronoun

In defining clauses, when the pronoun is the object, you can omit it:

The book (which/that) I read was amazing.
The man (whom/who/that) I met was friendly.

✓ Omitting is common in informal English and can make your writing more natural.

⚠️ BAC Exam Tips

  • Use "who" for people and "which" for things in non-defining clauses.
  • Never use "that" in non-defining clauses (with commas).
  • "Whom" is formal — it's good for BAC writing but optional in speech.
  • Use relative clauses to make your essays more sophisticated.
