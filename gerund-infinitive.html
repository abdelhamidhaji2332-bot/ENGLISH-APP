Gerund & Infinitive (المصدر و الفعل المجرد)

Gerund (V-ing) (المصدر)

A gerund is a verb form ending in -ing that functions as a noun. It can be a subject, object, or object of a preposition.

Verbs followed by Gerund

Common verbs: enjoy, avoid, suggest, recommend, finish, practice, mind, consider, postpone, admit, deny, imagine, miss, risk, involve, mention, quit, tolerate, understand

I enjoy reading novels.
She avoided going to the party.
He suggested taking a break.

After Prepositions

Always use a gerund after prepositions (in, on, at, of, for, about, etc.).

interested in learning English
good at playing football
thank you for coming
before leaving, check your bags

As Subject of a Sentence

Swimming is good exercise.
Studying regularly helps you pass the BAC exam.

Infinitive (to + V1) (الفعل المجرد)

The infinitive is the base form of the verb preceded by "to". It can function as a noun, adjective, or adverb.

Verbs followed by Infinitive

Common verbs: want, need, hope, plan, decide, promise, agree, refuse, expect, learn, offer, seem, appear, tend, pretend, manage, fail, deserve, afford

I want to go to university.
She hopes to pass the BAC exam.
They decided to move to a new city.

After Adjectives

happy to see you
difficult to understand
easy to use
important to remember

To Express Purpose

I went to the library to study.
She called me to invite me to her party.

Verbs That Take BOTH (with Meaning Change)

Verb+ Gerund+ Infinitive
stopstop doing (quit) — I stopped smoking.stop to do (pause for) — I stopped to smoke.
rememberremember doing (past) — I remember meeting him in 2020.remember to do (future) — Remember to call your mother.
forgetforget doing (past) — I'll never forget visiting Paris.forget to do (future) — Don't forget to lock the door.
trytry doing (experiment) — Try studying in the morning.try to do (attempt) — I tried to open the door but it was stuck.
regretregret doing (past) — I regret saying that.regret to say (formal) — I regret to inform you...

BAC Exam Tips

  • Memorize which verbs take gerund vs. infinitive — this is a common BAC question.
  • Use gerunds as subjects for more sophisticated sentence structure.
  • Be careful with verbs that change meaning (stop, remember, forget, try).
