Dialogue Writing (كتابة الحوار)

Dialogue tasks test your ability to use language functions naturally in conversation.

Dialogue Format

Topic: [Topic of conversation]
Characters: [Name A] and [Name B]
Setting: [Where and when]

[Character A]: [Line of dialogue]
[Character B]: [Response]
[Character A]: [Further dialogue]
[Character B]: [Response]

Key Functions in a Dialogue

A good BAC dialogue includes several of these functions:

FunctionExample
GreetingHi! How are you? / Good morning! Nice to see you!
Asking opinionWhat do you think about...?
Giving opinionIn my opinion... / I believe that...
AgreeingI agree with you. / That's a good point.
DisagreeingI see it differently. / I'm not sure I agree.
SuggestingHow about...? / Why don't we...?
ClosingIt was great talking to you. See you later!

Sample BAC Dialogue

Topic: Discussing the importance of recycling
Characters: Sara and Omar
Setting: School cafeteria

Sara: Hi Omar! You look busy reading that article. What is it about?
Omar: Hi Sara! It's about recycling. I think it's a very important topic.
Sara: I agree. Recycling helps protect the environment. What do you think about making it compulsory?
Omar: That's an interesting idea. In my opinion, it should be mandatory because it reduces waste and saves resources.
Sara: That's true. However, some people might find it difficult to change their habits.
Omar: You have a point. But if the government provides clear guidelines and bins, it would be easier.
Sara: I suggest we start a recycling campaign at our school to raise awareness.
Omar: That's a great idea! Let's talk to the principal about it tomorrow.
Sara: Perfect! I'll prepare a proposal. See you tomorrow!
Omar: See you! Good luck with the proposal.

BAC Tips

  • Include 3-5 exchanges per speaker.
  • Use a variety of language functions.
  • Make the dialogue natural and realistic.
  • Each speaker should have a distinct voice.
  • Use contractions (I'm, don't, let's) for natural speech.
